## Research boundaries

- Use external research to understand the employer, role context, and likely
  interview priorities—not to establish anything about the candidate.
- Keep company facts out of `career-master.md` and out of resume experience
  claims.
- Attach a source and research date to time-sensitive facts. Distinguish a
  sourced fact from an inference about what it may mean for the role.
- Do not infer confidential strategy, team structure, financial performance,
  or hiring intent from vague public signals.
- If the company name is ambiguous, resolve the legal entity, product, region,
  or business unit before researching.

## Research scope and sources

Research only the dimensions relevant to the application:

1. Products, customers, business model, and role-relevant business lines.
2. Stated values and observable operating principles. Do not present marketing
   language as proof of day-to-day culture.
3. Recent developments that could affect the role, normally from the preceding
   6-12 months unless an older event remains strategically important.
4. Public signals about the function, team interfaces, success measures, or
   business problems named in the JD.

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Official company pages, filings, investor materials, product documentation,
   and official announcements.
2. Government, regulator, exchange, or standards-body records.
3. Reputable reporting or research that adds context unavailable first-party.

For each source, retain the publisher, page title, publication date when
available, URL, and access or research date. Cite the supporting page next to
the claim; do not provide an undifferentiated source dump.

## Company-research output

Write `company-research.md` with:

1. Target company, role, business unit or region, and research date.
2. Role-relevant factual findings with inline citations.
3. Clearly labeled implications for resume emphasis or interview preparation.
4. Recent developments with event dates, not merely article dates.
5. Uncertainties, inaccessible sources, and claims that could not be verified.

Keep the research concise. Include only the subset useful for the application,
and do not copy general company history unless it affects the target role.

## Interview question design

Generate 10-15 questions by default:

- Roughly 50-60% behavioral questions derived from core responsibilities.
- Roughly 20-30% technical or professional questions derived from hard skills.
- Roughly 15-20% motivation and culture questions informed by the JD and
  sourced company context.

Treat these percentages as guidance, not quotas. Adjust for role type. For each
question, record:

- category and the JD requirement being tested;
- evidence state: `strong`, `partial`, `none`, or `needs-confirmation`;
- strongest verified experience and its career-master heading;
- concise answer outline or study points;
- verified facts or metrics worth mentioning;
- likely follow-up questions and an honesty-safe risk note.

Do not manufacture an answer for `none`. Explain the gap and prepare a truthful
bridge based on adjacent verified evidence. For `needs-confirmation`, state the
single fact whose confirmation would change the answer.

## STAR outline policy

Use STAR as an interview outline, not a memorized script:

- **Situation:** only the minimum verified context needed to understand the
  example.
- **Task:** the candidate's verified responsibility, target, or constraint.
- **Action:** two or three personally performed actions, including stakeholder
  coordination or decision steps only when verified.
- **Result:** the verified outcome, metric, artifact, or lesson. Do not invent a
  number to complete the structure.

An artifact such as an SOP, design, analysis, or project record is evidence only
when the user supplied it and it is safe to disclose. Never expose confidential
document names, links, customer information, or internal details merely to make
an answer appear stronger.

## Questions for the interviewer

Prepare 3-5 questions ordered by value. Favor questions about:

- the business problem and success measures for the role;
- team responsibilities and important interfaces;
- current priorities, constraints, and first-90-day expectations;
- decision-making, feedback, and operating practices.

Include the intent behind each question. Avoid questions answered by the cited
public sources. Keep compensation and benefits for the appropriate HR stage
unless the user explicitly wants negotiation preparation.

## Practical preparation

Include only useful, situation-aware reminders:

- confirmed interview format, location or video platform, time zone, duration,
  and materials to bring;
- communication or dress guidance supported by the known interview setting,
  role, or sourced company context, clearly labeled as general guidance when it
  is not company-specific;
- career gaps, short tenures, transitions, or hard-requirement gaps likely to
  need a concise truthful explanation;
- company facts and candidate metrics that should be rechecked immediately
  before the interview.

Avoid stereotypes based only on company category, country, age, or industry.

## Quality gate

- Cover the JD's highest-weight responsibilities and hard requirements.
- Trace every suggested experience and STAR fact to verified candidate data.
- Keep sourced company facts distinct from candidate facts and model analysis.
- Place citations beside current company claims and include the research date.
- Remove placeholders and unsupported claims from the final materials.
- Keep Markdown, DOCX, and PDF reader-facing content aligned when rendered.
- Visually inspect every rendered page and record unresolved gaps honestly.
